## 7. Bayesian calibration and physical-consistency loss

**Reviewer comment.** "The revised spectral-distance terminology is appropriate, but the claim that severity is not updated from healthy data remains inconsistent with the three-component state and severity output in the algorithm. Prior-mismatch classification results do not establish identifiability. Controlled parameter recovery, posterior predictive checks and coverage diagnostics remain necessary. The physical-consistency loss should explicitly identify its dependence on trainable parameters, its gradient path, reference quantities and application to non-bearing faults."

**Response.** The inconsistency is removed at the root: the calibration state is redefined as the two-component structural correction state [k, c]ᵀ (Sections 3.4.1, Algorithm 1 output, Nomenclature, Fig. 3 caption). Severity is a Stage-1 injection parameter of the simulator, not a filtered quantity; it cannot be inferred from healthy-condition data and is no longer part of the state-space formulation. The diagnostic head's severity output in Stage 3 is a classification posterior over severity-stratified classes, distinct from the filtered state, and the manuscript says so.

Identifiability. We agree that prior-mismatch results are robustness evidence only; Appendix E.1 now says exactly that. Appendix E.2 is new and implements the three requested diagnostics on the [k, c] state: controlled parameter recovery over a ±20 % grid of true corrections with noise-matched synthetic observations (correlation and RMSE of recovered versus true, per component); posterior predictive checks comparing the predictive distribution of band-limited spectral features with observations (MMD and residual-structure inspection); and frequentist coverage of the 50/90/95 % posterior credible intervals across the grid (Table E.2 of the revised manuscript). Section 3.4.1 and the limitations (Section 6.3) state that identifiability is characterised by these diagnostics rather than assumed.

Physical-consistency loss. Section 3.8 is new. It defines L_phy(Θ) = Σ_c w_c (Ê_Θ(f_c)/E_c^{ref} − 1)², identifies the trainable parameters Θ (feature extractor, attention module, diagnostic head), states the full gradient path ∂L_phy/∂Θ through the differentiable attention and feature maps, specifies the reference quantities E_c^{ref} as analytically predicted band energies computed from the simulator geometry and the measured speed (constants with respect to Θ, receiving no gradient), and enumerates the constraint set: bearing BPFI/BPFO/BSF/FTF bands; gear-mesh frequency and its sidebands for gear faults; the 2× order for misalignment and the 1× order for imbalance. It also states the scope boundary explicitly: broadband signatures such as propeller cavitation are outside L_phy, so the constraint set is empty there, and single-fault spectral constraints do not encode fault-coupling physics — one reason composite faults remain weakest.
