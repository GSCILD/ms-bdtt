# 4. Experimental setup

## 4.1 Datasets and provenance

Experiments use six datasets spanning controlled laboratory conditions to simulated marine deployments, which provides a validation hierarchy of increasing realism. All dataset metadata in this section have been re-verified against the providers' documentation, and the complete provenance — exact file identifiers, channels, native sampling rates, operating conditions, label definitions, exclusions and preprocessing — is documented in Appendix F and shipped as machine-readable manifests with checksums in the reproducibility archive (Code and Data Availability).

CWRU. The Case Western Reserve University bearing dataset contains accelerometer measurements from a drivetrain test rig and is among the most widely used benchmarks for bearing fault diagnosis [36]. Drive-end accelerometer signals sampled at 12 kHz are used throughout; fan-end and 48 kHz recordings are excluded. The label set comprises four fault categories — normal, inner-race (IR), ball and outer-race (OR) defects — at three defect diameters (0.007, 0.014 and 0.021 inch), giving ten class labels in total, recorded under four motor loads (0, 1, 2 and 3 HP, corresponding to 1797, 1772, 1750 and 1730 r/min). After sliding-window segmentation approximately 30,000 samples are available (exact per-class window counts in Table F.1).

XJTU-SY. This rolling-element bearing run-to-failure dataset was acquired on a bearing test rig under three operating conditions: 2100 r/min with a 12 kN radial load, 2250 r/min with 11 kN, and 2400 r/min with 10 kN [37]. (The operating speeds stated in an earlier version of this manuscript — 2100, 1500 and 900 r/min — were incorrect; the provider specifies 2100, 2250 and 2400 r/min, and the manuscript has been corrected accordingly.) Vibration signals are sampled at 25.6 kHz in segments of 1.28 s (32,768 samples) every minute, from two accelerometers (horizontal-radial and axial). Thirty-five run-to-failure tests are provided; the terminal damage mode of each test is documented by the provider's inspection report and spans inner-race, outer-race, roller/rolling-element and combination damages in varying combinations. Windows are labelled with the explicit degradation-boundary protocol of Section 4.2.3; after labelling and exclusions, the labelled pool comprises approximately 28,000 windows (Table F.2).

Paderborn. The Paderborn bearing dataset [38] provides vibration and motor-current measurements from a module-based test rig, synchronously sampled at 64 kHz. For each bearing and operating condition, 20 measurements of 4 s each are recorded. Four operating conditions are provided: 1500 r/min with 0.7 N·m load torque and 1000 N radial force (baseline); 900 r/min with 0.7 N·m and 1000 N; 1500 r/min with 0.1 N·m and 1000 N; and 1500 r/min with 0.7 N·m and 400 N. (The description in an earlier version — four load levels at a constant 1500 r/min with twelve accelerometer channels — was incorrect and has been replaced by the provider's specification.) The housing-vibration channel measured in the main radial-load direction is used. Thirty-two bearings are provided: six healthy (K001–K006), fourteen with real damage from accelerated lifetime tests (outer-race codes KA04, KA15, KA16, KA22, KA30; inner-race codes KI04, KI14, KI16, KI18, KI21; combined codes KB23, KB27, KB29, KB33) and twelve with artificial damage. Only the healthy and real-damage bearings are used; artificial-damage bearings are excluded because their machining signatures differ physically from service-type damage. After segmentation, the pool comprises 14,700 candidate windows, of which 12,600 remain after excluding the early-stage damage categories that lack counterparts in the other datasets (the removed categories account for exactly one seventh of the candidate pool; per-class counts in Table F.3). This reconciles the window counts quoted at different stages of the review process.

SEU. The Southeast University dataset [39] was collected on a drivetrain dynamics simulator comprising a motor, a planetary gearbox, a fixed-axis reduction gearbox and a magnetic brake. Signals are sampled at 5120 Hz with eight channels per recording: motor vibration, three planetary-gearbox accelerations (x, y, z), motor torque, and three reduction-gearbox accelerations (x, y, z). Two operating conditions are provided: 20 Hz input speed with 0 V brake command (0 N·m) and 30 Hz with 2 V (7.32 N·m). This study uses the gearbox (gear-fault) subset, whose five health states are healthy, chipped tooth, missing tooth, root fault (crack at the tooth root) and surface wear; each condition provides the five classes as separate recordings. (The description in an earlier version — six fault types under three load levels of 0, 10 and 20 N·m sampled at 10 kHz from proximity probes — was incorrect and has been replaced by the provider's specification.) The three reduction-gearbox acceleration channels and the motor-vibration channel are used. After segmentation approximately 12,000 samples are available (Table F.4). The dataset is valuable for cross-equipment transfer because its gear-fault physics differs from the bearing physics of CWRU, XJTU-SY and Paderborn.

MarineSim (custom high-fidelity simulator). MarineSim is a high-fidelity marine propulsion simulator that implements a parameterised rotordynamic model of the crankshaft–gearbox–shaft-line system of a geared medium-speed four-stroke propulsion diesel engine of the MAN 48/60CR class [40], together with a thermo-mechanical engine model and a propeller–hull interaction model based on published resistance and propulsion data [14]. Model validation compares simulated natural frequencies and fault characteristic frequencies with published measurements for comparable shaft-line systems; agreement is within 5 % for natural frequencies and 2 % for fault frequencies. Five fault types (bearing outer-race defect, shaft crack, gear-tooth spalling, coupling misalignment and imbalance) are injected over a continuous operating envelope of 350–600 r/min and 30–100 % load. Approximately 20,000 samples are generated with marine sensor-noise characteristics as described in Section 3.2.

SPFD (synthetic ship propulsion fault dataset). SPFD is generated with MarineSim to emulate the vibration signatures of the propulsion plant of a 4,500-TEU container vessel. Sea-state conditions (wave height 0.5–3 m, wind speed 5–15 m/s) and propeller-induced excitations are modelled following established marine hydrodynamics references [14]. The dataset contains approximately 5,000 samples with simulated sea-induced noise, hull vibration and propeller excitation, organised into four primary fault categories — bearing wear (early, moderate and severe), shaft misalignment, propeller cavitation damage and gear pitting — together with a composite-fault group and a separate pool of normal-condition samples reserved for calibration. Per-group class supports are listed in Table F.5. The figure of approximately 7,000 samples quoted in a previous response document included the auxiliary sea-state-augmented calibration pool; it is not part of the evaluation dataset, and the manuscript now states 5,000 consistently, with the composition fixed by the generation manifest. As noted in Section 6.3, SPFD is synthetic: it enables controlled and reproducible evaluation under realistic marine conditions, but it does not substitute for measurements from an operational vessel.

## 4.2 Scenario design

Five scenarios of increasing difficulty progressively remove the information available for transfer. For every scenario, this section states the source and target sets, the label space with its cross-dataset mapping, the supervision available to the methods, and the evaluation track. All window counts, class supports and split sizes are generated from the split manifests in the reproducibility archive.

### 4.2.1 S1 (cross-load)

The source domain is CWRU data recorded at 3 HP (1730 r/min) and the target domain is CWRU data recorded at 1 HP (1772 r/min). The load change along the propeller curve mimics the load changes experienced by marine engines. The label space is the full ten-class CWRU set on both sides; no cross-dataset mapping is required. This scenario tests sensitivity to moderate load changes and is used for hyperparameter selection and ablation; it shares no records with the other scenarios.

### 4.2.2 S2 (cross-speed, same-recording resample under record-disjoint partitioning)

The source domain is CWRU data recorded at 0 HP (1797 r/min) and the target domain is the same recording order-tracked and resampled to 1200 r/min, a condition that is not among the native CWRU operating speeds (1797, 1772, 1750, 1730 r/min) but emulates a roughly 33 % speed reduction through propeller slip and sea-state variation; the resampling is stated explicitly because the target condition is synthesised rather than measured. Because the target is a transformation of the source recordings, the partition rule of Section 4.4 is applied with specific force: partitions are assigned at the raw-record level before any transformation, and every derivative window — order-tracked, resampled or otherwise transformed — inherits the partition membership of its parent record. Consequently, a raw signal and all of its transformed derivatives always remain in the same partition: the S2 target test set contains only derivatives of test-partition records, no derivative of a training-partition record enters the test set, and no test-partition record (raw or derived) is used for training, calibration, normalisation statistics or model selection. The label space is the ten-class CWRU set on both sides.

### 4.2.3 S3 (cross-dataset with explicit degradation labelling)

The source domain is CWRU bearing data and the target domain is XJTU-SY bearing data. Different equipment types, fault mechanisms and sensor configurations create a substantial domain shift, so this scenario evaluates transfer across fundamentally different rotating machinery.

Label space and mapping. The mapped label space is {healthy, inner-race, outer-race, roller}: CWRU {normal, IR, OR, ball} maps one-to-one onto XJTU-SY {healthy pre-onset windows, terminal IR damage, terminal OR damage, terminal roller damage}. XJTU-SY trajectories whose terminal inspection reports a combination damage (for example IR+OR) are excluded from S3, because the CWRU source has no combination class; this exclusion is stated rather than silently absorbed (trajectory counts in Table F.2).

Ground truth and severity boundaries. Terminal bearing-failure labels are not automatically assigned to all preceding windows. Each run-to-failure trajectory is processed by an explicit three-step protocol. Step 1 (baseline): the first 60 windows of each trajectory define a per-trajectory baseline (mean and standard deviation of band-limited RMS). Step 2 (onset): the degradation onset index t* is the first window index at which the rolling RMS exceeds baseline mean + 3σ; windows before t* are labelled healthy. Step 3 (definite damage): windows after the index t** at which the rolling RMS exceeds baseline mean + 5σ are labelled with the terminal damage mode documented by the provider's inspection report; the interval (t*, t**] is treated as ambiguous — it is neither confidently healthy nor confidently faulted — and is excluded from training, validation and evaluation. Severity boundaries for the severity-stratified analyses of Section 5.4 are defined by RMS quantiles within the definite-damage segment (lower third: early; middle third: moderate; upper third: severe). The protocol is deterministic, uses only the provider's inspection reports and the signals themselves, and is reproduced by the regeneration scripts; the resulting window counts per class are listed in Table F.2, and the S3 results of Table 5 correspond to this protocol after regeneration.

### 4.2.4 S4 (cross-equipment with source-private and target-private classes)

The source domain is Paderborn bearing data and the target domain is SEU gearbox (gear-fault) data. Bearings and gears produce qualitatively different vibration signatures — impulsive as opposed to mesh-periodic — and the framework must learn equipment-invariant defect representations.

Label space and mapping. The two label spaces only partially overlap, and S4 is therefore an open-set cross-equipment scenario rather than a partial-domain adaptation scenario (in the strict sense of partial adaptation, the target label set is a subset of the source label set, which is not the case here). The mapped three-class space is {healthy, crack-type defect, surface-type defect}: Paderborn {healthy, inner-race defect, outer-race defect} maps onto SEU {healthy, root fault, chipped tooth}, by the crack-type correspondence of the inner-race defect and the tooth-root crack and the surface/spall-type correspondence of the outer-race defect and the chipped tooth. The Paderborn combined IR+OR class is source-private (no SEU counterpart), and the SEU missing-tooth and surface-wear classes are target-private (no Paderborn counterpart). The mapping is a stated working assumption justified by damage-geometry correspondence; its sensitivity is examined by the open-set track below, which does not depend on the mapping.

Evaluation tracks. Two tracks are reported. (i) Closed-set track: accuracy restricted to the three mapped classes; the S4 column of Table 5 follows this track after regeneration. (ii) Open-set track: the classifier is trained on the mapped source classes and must additionally reject target-private samples as unknown. The rejection rule is: a target query is rejected as unknown when its maximum softmax probability falls below τ_rej, where τ_rej is the 95th percentile of the maximum-softmax distribution on held-out source-validation data; the threshold is therefore selected without any target label and is fixed across all methods. Open-set metrics are closed-set accuracy over mapped classes (CCA), unknown-detection AUROC, open-set accuracy OS (unknown rejected), and OS* (unknown treated as its own class). Results of the open-set track for MS-BDTT, COSDA, DANN and Source-Only are reported in Section 5.4.

### 4.2.5 S5 (unlabelled-target marine with explicit supervision disclosure)

The source domains are CWRU, XJTU-SY, Paderborn and SEU, all labelled, and the target domain is SPFD, which is unlabelled. No labelled fault data from the marine domain are available during training, and only unlabelled normal-condition samples from SPFD are used for Bayesian calibration. This scenario represents the most practically relevant setting for marine power fault diagnosis.

Severity mapping and its justification. Laboratory defect sizes and degradation stages are related to marine wear severity through an explicit defect-area-fraction mapping. The severity index of the simulator is the defect area as a fraction of the load-bearing cross-section (η, s ∈ [0, 0.3], Appendix A). The laboratory evidence is mapped as follows: CWRU defect diameters 0.007, 0.014 and 0.021 inch correspond to defect-area fractions of approximately 0.5 %, 1.9 % and 4.4 % of the raceway load-bearing section, which fall in the lower, middle and upper parts of the simulator's early-to-moderate range; XJTU-SY terminal damage states and Paderborn real-damage extents, quantified by their RMS-severity quantiles under the protocol of Section 4.2.3, cover the moderate-to-severe range; and the SEU gear-fault classes (chipped, missing, root, surface) map onto the gear-pitting severity ladder by the same geometry-based correspondence used in S4. The mapping is a modelling assumption, not a measurement; it is stated in full in Table F.6, and the severity-stratified results of Table 8 are interpreted with that caveat.

Supervision disclosure. Two SPFD target categories — shaft misalignment and propeller cavitation — have no labelled counterpart in any of the four public datasets. The class identity for these categories is supplied exclusively by simulator-generated bridge prototypes, that is, labelled synthetic samples produced by MarineSim at operating points along the interpolation path. This supervision is stated explicitly, and it is made consistently available to all baselines: every method in Table 5 receives the same calibrated bridge set, including the prototype-derived labels for misalignment and cavitation, in addition to the four public source datasets. No method receives any additional target-domain information. The demand map and bridge construction use source-training and simulator data only (Section 4.4).

## 4.3 Baseline methods and evaluation metrics

MS-BDTT is evaluated against ten baseline methods spanning five methodological categories.

No adaptation. Source-Only trains a classifier on the labelled source data and applies it directly to the target without any adaptation; it indicates what can be achieved without transfer learning.

Statistical domain adaptation. Deep CORAL aligns second-order statistics between source and target features [17]; MMD-JDA combines feature adaptation with classifier adaptation under MMD regularisation [18]. These methods represent the moment-matching paradigm.

Adversarial domain adaptation. DANN learns domain-invariant features through a gradient reversal layer [19]; CDAN extends DANN with conditional adversarial losses and entropy minimisation for more discriminative features [21].

Simulation-based methods. Sim-Only trains on mechanistic simulation data directly, without calibration; RandomBridge generates bridge samples at uniformly spaced operating points, without demand-guided selection or attention.

Digital twin methods. DT-Only uses a calibrated digital twin for prediction without cross-domain transfer; DT+DA combines digital twin simulation with domain adaptation but without the continuous bridge and the attention mechanism.

State-of-the-art methods. PI-DA integrates physics-informed constraints into domain adaptation [20]; COSDA combines optimal transport with adversarial learning and, among the baselines considered, is the best-performing method on benchmark cross-domain fault diagnosis tasks.

Fairness of supervision. All methods are trained and evaluated on identical inputs under each scenario: the same split manifests, the same labelled source data, the same calibrated bridge set (including the prototype-derived supervision of Section 4.2.5 in S5), and the same rejection threshold in the S4 open-set track. Baseline hyperparameters are retuned on the S1 validation split only, under the same budget as MS-BDTT (Appendix D).

Evaluation metrics. Four metrics are reported, each defined once and used consistently. (i) Overall accuracy (OA): the sample-weighted percentage of correctly classified test samples; when a macro-average is meant — the unweighted mean over classes or over scenarios — it is labelled as such at the point of use. (ii) The per-class F1-score, computed as the harmonic mean of precision and recall for each fault type; class supports for every reported aggregate are given in Appendix F. (iii) The MMD-based domain gap: the V-statistic MMD² estimate of Eq. (12), computed on the 512-dimensional penultimate features with a Gaussian RBF kernel whose length-scale is set by the median heuristic on pooled features, with per-domain sample sizes stated in Appendix C.4. (iv) The computational cost, expressed as training time and inference latency measured on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

## 4.4 Partitioning and leakage control

Generalisation setting. The intended generalisation setting is record-disjoint cross-condition adaptation: models are trained on records from some operating conditions or datasets and evaluated on records from other conditions or datasets that share no records with the training set. Within each public dataset, the unit of separation is the smallest unit that can be considered statistically independent: the measurement record for CWRU (each 4 s recording of the drive-end file set), the run-to-failure trajectory for XJTU-SY (all windows of one bearing are assigned to a single partition, so that no bearing contributes windows to both sides), the bearing specimen for Paderborn (all recordings of one bearing, across all operating conditions, remain together), and the condition-recording file for SEU. Because file-level separation does not by itself guarantee independence between bearings, assets or run-to-failure trajectories, these unit choices are stated explicitly, and the split manifests record, for every window, its parent record, parent bearing or trajectory, dataset and partition.

Partition protocol. Partitions are assigned before any windowing, resampling or transformation. For S2, every derivative window inherits the partition of its parent record (Section 4.2.2). Calibration, normalisation, demand-map construction and model selection are all governed by the same restrictions: (i) per-domain normalisation statistics are computed on the training partition only; (ii) the 120 unlabelled SPFD normal-condition samples used by the Bayesian calibration belong to a dedicated calibration partition, disjoint from the SPFD test partition; (iii) the demand map and the bridge samples are constructed from source-training and simulator data only — no target-domain data enter them; (iv) all hyperparameters and model selection decisions are made on the S1 validation split and then frozen for S2–S5; and (v) no test-partition record is used at any stage of training, calibration, normalisation or selection. The split manifests (CSV files with SHA-256 checksums, one row per window) are deposited in the reproducibility archive together with a leakage-check script that re-verifies items (i)–(v) from the manifests alone.

## 4.5 Implementation details

All hyperparameters were selected by grid search on the S1 validation split to ensure a fair comparison (Appendix D). The demand-map weight is α = 0.6, the particle filter uses N = 500 particles with systematic resampling at ESS < 250 and a likelihood scale σ = 0.1 calibrated from healthy baseline data, and the attention mechanism uses a hidden dimension d_h = 256 with temperature τ = 1.0. The joint loss uses λ = 0.5, ramped from 0 to its final value over epochs 1–20, and μ = 0.1. All models are implemented in PyTorch 2.0 with CUDA 11.8 and trained with the Adam optimizer at an initial learning rate of 10⁻³ under a cosine annealing schedule; the batch size is 64 for all methods. Unless stated otherwise, the main experiments use a bridge budget of 120 samples per operating condition with K = 9 interpolation nodes; Section 5.7 varies this budget. Following common practice in adversarial domain adaptation, the learning-rate schedule is restarted at the beginning of each adaptation epoch. The full evaluation grid of Tables 5, 7 and 8 uses three independent random seeds (42, 43, 44), set through torch.manual_seed, torch.cuda.manual_seed_all and numpy.random.seed; data shuffling uses the same seeds so that the train/test splits are identical for all methods. Because three runs cannot support confirmatory claims, the headline comparison (MS-BDTT versus COSDA, all five scenarios) is additionally replicated with ten independent seeds, and all grid-level comparisons are labelled exploratory (Section 5.6).

## 4.6 Statistical protocol

Run-level uncertainty. Every mean is reported with the standard deviation over training runs (runs differ in seed, initialisation and shuffling only). Aggregate columns never mix dispersion types: in Table 5, the value after ± in the Average column is the across-scenario standard deviation of the five per-scenario means — a dispersion indicator across conditions of difficulty, not run-to-run uncertainty — and is marked with a footnote; run-to-run uncertainty is reported per scenario and in Table 9.

Bootstrap. The resampling unit of the bootstrap is the test record (the cluster of windows derived from one measurement record), resampled with replacement in a class-stratified fashion within each training run; the bootstrap therefore preserves the within-record correlation of windows and the class composition. For each training run, a 95 % confidence interval of the accuracy or of the difference in accuracy is computed from B = 1,000 record-level resamples; across runs, the per-run intervals are reported as a range, and the interval of the mean difference is computed from the run-level paired differences (below), not by pooling test samples across runs.

Paired comparisons. The primary inferential quantity for the MS-BDTT-versus-COSDA comparison is the paired difference at run level: runs are paired by seed, the differences ΔOA are computed per seed, and the two-sided paired t-test over the n seeds, the bootstrap CI of the mean paired difference (unit: seed, B = 10,000), and Cohen's d for paired data (using the standard deviation of the paired differences) are reported. With three seeds, the t-test has two degrees of freedom and is exploratory; the ten-seed replication of Section 5.6 is the confirmatory analysis for the headline comparison. We note for transparency that the S5 p-value of 0.009 printed in a previous version is not reproducible from the displayed three-run statistics, whose minimum compatible two-sided p-value is approximately 0.0138; the corrected value is reported in Table 9.
