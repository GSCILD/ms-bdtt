# Part I. Response to the Editor

**Editor comment 1. "While the revision has improved several aspects and two reviewers have shown a positive response, significant concerns remain regarding experimental validity, numerical consistency, statistical analysis, data partitioning, and reproducibility."**

*Response.* We address the five named concerns as follows, with the detailed point-by-point responses in Part II.

(1) *Experimental validity.* The dataset metadata have been re-verified against the providers' documentation and corrected where they were wrong: the XJTU-SY operating speeds (now 2100/2250/2400 r/min with 12/11/10 kN), the Paderborn operating conditions and channels (now the provider's four conditions at 900/1500 r/min, 0.1/0.7 N·m, 400/1000 N, one housing-vibration channel at 64 kHz), and the SEU dataset (now the drivetrain dynamics simulator, two conditions 20 Hz–0 V and 30 Hz–2 V, 5.12 kHz, eight channels, five gear-fault classes). Full provenance tables with file identifiers, channels, labels, exclusions and preprocessing are new (Section 4.1, Appendix F). The S2 same-recording construction is reconciled with record-disjoint evaluation through an explicit derivative-inheritance rule (Sections 4.2.2, 4.4). The S3 labelling, previously an uncontrolled propagation of terminal labels, is replaced by a three-step degradation-boundary protocol with explicit ground-truth and severity boundaries (Section 4.2.3). (3) *Statistical analysis.* The evaluation grid is now explicitly labelled exploratory (three runs). (4) *Data partitioning.* The intended generalisation setting is now stated (record-disjoint cross-condition adaptation, with per-dataset separation units), and the partition restrictions are extended to normalisation, calibration, demand-map construction and model selection (Section 4.4). Split manifests with checksums, including the transformed S2 records, ship with the archive together with a leakage-check script.

(5) *Reproducibility.* The complete package — code for all stages and baselines, manifests, seeds, baseline configurations, weights, run-level predictions, raw MMD logs and one regeneration script per table and figure — is deposited in a Zenodo archive with a resolvable DOI and linked from a dedicated Code and data availability section (manuscript, "Code and data availability").

## 1. Dataset provenance and scenario validity

Identifying S2 as synthetically resampled is appropriate. However, the use of the 'same recording' must be reconciled with record-disjoint evaluation: a raw signal and all its transformed derivatives must remain in the same partition."

**Response.** All three corrections are implemented. (i) XJTU-SY: the manuscript now states 2100, 2250 and 2400 r/min with 12, 11 and 10 kN, and explicitly notes the correction of the earlier erroneous speeds (Sections 4.1, F.2). (ii) Paderborn: re-verified against the provider's documentation — four operating conditions (1500 r/min/0.7 N·m/1000 N; 900 r/min/0.7 N·m/1000 N; 1500 r/min/0.1 N·m/1000 N; 1500 r/min/0.7 N·m/400 N), synchronous 64 kHz acquisition, 20 measurements of 4 s per bearing and condition, one housing-vibration channel in the main radial-load direction; the previous "four load levels at constant 1500 r/min with twelve accelerometer channels" was wrong and is corrected (Sections 4.1, F.3). (iii) SEU: re-verified — drivetrain dynamics simulator; two conditions (20 Hz–0 V, i.e. 0 N·m; 30 Hz–2 V, i.e. 7.32 N·m); 5.12 kHz; eight channels (motor vibration; planetary-gearbox x/y/z; motor torque; reduction-gearbox x/y/z); five gear-fault classes (healthy, chipped tooth, missing tooth, root fault, surface wear); the previous "six fault types under three load levels (0, 10, 20 N·m) at 10 kHz from proximity probes" was wrong and is corrected (Sections 4.1, F.4).

Provenance. Appendix F is new. For each dataset it states the exact file identifiers (CWRU drive-end 12 kHz file sets with the fan-end/48 kHz exclusion; XJTU-SY trajectory directories 35Hz12kN / 37.5Hz11kN / 40Hz10kN with per-bearing sequential 1.28 s segments; Paderborn bearing codes K001–K006, KA04/KA15/KA16/KA22/KA30, KI04/KI14/KI16/KI18/KI21, KB23/KB27/KB29/KB33, artificial-damage bearings excluded; SEU per-class-per-condition recording files), the channels and native sampling rates, the operating conditions, the label definitions, the exclusions (including the Paderborn early-stage categories and the XJTU-SY combination-damage trajectories) and the preprocessing chain. S2. The partition rule now assigns partitions at the raw-record level before any transformation, and every derivative window — order-tracked, resampled or otherwise transformed — inherits its parent record's partition. A raw signal and all of its transformed derivatives therefore always remain in the same partition; the S2 test set contains only derivatives of test-partition records, and no derivative of a training-partition record is used for training, calibration, normalisation or selection (Sections 4.2.2, 4.4). The S2 manifests in the archive record the inheritance explicitly, and the regenerated S2 split yields the S2 column of Table 5. ## 2. Cross-dataset label compatibility

**Reviewer comment.** "The new mapping table is helpful, but the resulting tasks remain insufficiently justified. For S3, terminal bearing-failure labels cannot automatically be assigned to all preceding windows; explicit ground truth and severity boundaries are needed. S4 contains both source-private and target-private classes, so its description as partial-domain adaptation requires reconsideration. The rejection rule, threshold selection and promised unknown-class results must be specified. For S5, laboratory defect sizes and degradation stages need a defensible relationship to marine wear severity. If simulated bridge prototypes supply target-only class identities, this supervision should be explicit and consistently available to the baselines."

**Response.**

S3. The revised Section 4.2.3 replaces terminal-label propagation with a three-step, deterministic protocol: (1) per-trajectory baseline from the first 60 windows; (2) degradation onset t* at rolling-RMS > baseline + 3σ, before which windows are healthy; (3) definite damage t** at rolling-RMS > baseline + 5σ, after which windows carry the provider's inspected terminal damage mode; the interval (t*, t**] is ambiguous and is excluded from training, validation and evaluation. Severity boundaries for the severity-stratified analyses are RMS tertiles within the definite-damage segment. The provider's inspection reports are the ground truth for the damage mode; the signal-based thresholds define the boundaries; nothing is propagated silently. The label budget under this protocol is reported in Table F.2 (per-trajectory and per-class counts), and Table 5's S3 column corresponds to this protocol after regeneration.

S4. We agree, and the description is corrected. With the verified metadata, the Paderborn label set is {healthy, inner-race, outer-race, combined} and the SEU gear set is {healthy, chipped, missing, root, surface}; the mapped three-class space is {healthy, crack-type, surface-type} via the stated damage-geometry correspondence (inner-race ↔ root fault; outer-race ↔ chipped tooth), the Paderborn combined class is source-private, and the SEU missing-tooth and surface-wear classes are target-private. S4 is therefore an open-set cross-equipment scenario, not a partial-domain adaptation (the target label set is not a subset of the source set). The rejection rule is: reject as unknown when the maximum softmax probability falls below τ_rej, with τ_rej fixed at the 95th percentile of the maximum-softmax distribution on held-out source-validation data — no target label touches the threshold. The promised unknown-class results are now a dedicated evaluation track (closed-set accuracy, unknown-detection AUROC, OS, OS*) reported in Table 8b for MS-BDTT, COSDA, DANN and Source-Only (CCA, unknown-detection AUROC, OS and OS* for all four methods), with the threshold rule fixed across all methods (Sections 4.2.4, 5.4.1).

S5. The severity relationship is now an explicit, documented mapping (Table F.6): the simulator's severity index is the defect area fraction of the load-bearing cross-section (0–30 %); CWRU defect diameters map to area fractions of ≈0.5 %, 1.9 % and 4.4 % (early/moderate bands); XJTU-SY and Paderborn damages enter through RMS-severity tertiles of Section 4.2.3; SEU gear classes map onto the gear-pitting ladder by the S4 geometry correspondence. The manuscript states that this mapping is a modelling assumption, not a measurement, and interprets the severity-stratified results accordingly (Sections 4.2.5, 5.4, 6.3). Prototype-derived supervision: the shaft-misalignment and propeller-cavitation class identities are supplied exclusively by simulator bridge prototypes; this is stated explicitly in Section 4.2.5, and Section 4.3 guarantees that every baseline receives the identical calibrated bridge set including that supervision — no method receives anything the others do not.

*Changes.* Sections 4.2.3–4.2.5, 4.3, 5.4, 5.4.1 (new), 6.3; Tables 4, 8b (new), F.2, F.6 (new).

Paderborn counts.
